$2,499 when you want five to eight pages with the copy written for you, reviews, local schema and analytics. That is the right price when you want each service to rank on its own.

Pay more only for a custom tool (instant quote calculator, booking bot, missed-call-to-text) at $4,999 starting, or for e-commerce and deep integrations, which I quote separately. Every project gets a fixed price and a date in writing first.

Type the question into Google and page one is ten posts from ten companies that sell websites. Every one of them gives you a range. The low end is a template you fill in yourself, the high end is a five-figure agency build, and somewhere in the middle is the tier they would like to sell you. Not one of them says: pay this.

I understand why. A range is safe. A number can be argued with. But you are a plumber trying to decide what to spend before the slow season, and a range that spans two orders of magnitude is not advice. So here is a number, and the arithmetic behind it.

Why page one will not give you a number

Each post on page one is written by a company that sells websites. The range it quotes is that company's own price list with the name filed off. When a post says a custom plumbing site runs several thousand dollars up front and a few hundred a month after, what it is telling you is that this is what that company charges, and that the monthly fee is where the margin lives.

That is not dishonest. It is just not an answer to your question. Your question is what a plumbing company in Grand Rapids should spend to get a site that makes the phone ring, and the answer depends on what the site has to do, not on what the person answering happens to sell.

Look at who is answering. Of the five posts I opened, three end by pricing the author's own monthly plan, one is a UK post from 2022 quoting in pounds, and every one of them is a seller. That is the page-one answer to "how much should I pay": whatever the person you happen to be reading charges.

I sell websites too, so read the rest with that in mind. The difference is that my prices are published, every project gets a fixed quote, and I will tell you when the cheap one is the wrong one.

The arithmetic for a plumbing site

A plumbing website has one job: a person with water on the floor finds you, believes you, and taps the phone number. Everything else is in service of that.

Count what that actually takes. A home page that says what you do and where. A services page, or several. A contact page with the number, a form and your hours. That is three pages. Add reviews pulled onto the page, a service-area list of the suburbs you actually drive to, and the schema markup that tells Google you are a plumber in Grand Rapids and not a plumbing supply store, and you have a complete site.

That is what $999 buys, and it is why I can build it in days rather than months: the design is not the hard part of a three-page trades site, and I write the copy so it does not sit in your inbox waiting for a free weekend.

The step up to $2,499 is about pages, and pages are about search. If you want to show up for "water heater replacement Grand Rapids" and also "sewer line repair" and also "emergency plumber," each of those needs its own page with its own copy, and Google needs to be told about each one. Five to eight pages, written and marked up, is a different amount of work, and the price reflects the work rather than the label.

The price of a plumbing site should track the number of pages it needs, not the number of people at the agency who need to be paid.

What moves the price, and when $999 is not enough

Four things change the quote, and I will tell you which way each one pushes it.

  • Number of pages. The main driver. Three pages is a Starter Site. Eight pages is a Business Site. Twenty pages for a company that does plumbing, heating and electrical across three counties is a custom quote, and a fair one, because each page is written and checked.
  • Whether you need a tool, and how many systems it talks to. A form that emails you is included. An instant quote calculator, a booking bot that fills your calendar, or missed-call-to-text is the Growth tier. A tool that has to read and write your dispatch software, your invoicing and your CRM at once costs more, because every connection is work and every connection can break.
  • Whether the content exists. If you have photos of your trucks and your crew, a price list, and a clear offer ("same-day drain cleaning, upfront pricing"), the project moves fast. If none of that exists yet, I draft from your Google listing and reviews, but it is slower and it shows in the quote.
  • The deadline. Days is normal for a Starter Site. Faster than that is sometimes possible and costs more.

Be honest with yourself about three cases where the $999 tier is the wrong choice. If you need more than three pages to say what you do, it is a Business Site. If the site has to plug into scheduling, payments or a CRM, it is at least the Growth tier. And if you sell parts or fixtures online, that is e-commerce, which none of my listed tiers includes; I will quote it, but it will not be $999 and anyone who tells you it will be is quoting a template.

How to decide: three questions

Answer these before you talk to anyone, including me.

  1. How many services do you want to rank for separately? If the answer is "plumbing," it is a Starter Site at $999. If the answer is a list (water heaters, drains, sewer, remodels, emergency), each one is a page, and it is a Business Site at $2,499.
  2. Does the site need to do anything besides make the phone ring? If the phone ringing is the whole goal, stop at Business. If you are losing jobs because nobody answers at seven in the evening, missed-call-to-text or a booking bot is worth the Growth price, and not before.
  3. Do you already have the content? Photos, a price list, a clear offer. If yes, the quote will sit near the starting price. If no, budget for photography separately and let me draft the words; do not let the project wait on you.

Common questions

How much does a plumber website cost in Grand Rapids?

Is a $999 website good enough for a plumbing company?

For most one-truck or two-truck shops, yes. Three pages that say what you do, where you do it and how to reach you, with a tappable phone number and a form, is a complete plumbing site. It stops being enough when you want separate pages for each service so they rank on their own, when the site has to connect to scheduling, payments or a CRM, or when you sell parts online. Those are the Business and Growth tiers, or a custom quote for e-commerce.

Why do agencies quote up to $15,000 for a plumber website?

Because the ranges on page one are each seller's own price list, and an agency price includes a team: an account manager, a designer, a developer and a project manager who all bill for talking to each other. The sources quoted in this article put an agency or custom build at $5,000 to $15,000 and up, a freelancer build at $1,000 to $6,000, and the monthly plans at $150 to $547 a month. A one-builder shop does the same three to eight pages without the overhead, which is the whole difference. Pay the agency price when you need a team, such as for e-commerce or a multi-location build.
