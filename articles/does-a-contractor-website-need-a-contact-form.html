Does a contractor website need a contact form? Yes, a short one — 23 of 39 Grand Rapids map-pack winners put one on the home page, median six fields

Page one for this question is ten agency blogs telling you what a form “should” ask, and none of them counted anything. On 16 September 2026 I read the home pages of the 39 trade sites that hold the top map spots in Grand Rapids and counted the forms, the fields, and every other way a visitor is given to reach them.

By Matthew Kerr Published 16 Sep 2026 Updated 16 Sep 2026 9 min read
Quick answer

Yes — alongside a tap-to-call link, not instead of one. Of the 39 top-ranked Grand Rapids plumbers, roofers, HVAC and electrical home pages read 16 September 2026, 23 carry a lead form on the home page itself; 13 more link to a contact page without one; 32 have a tap-to-call link; 7 an email link. Three show no form, no contact link, no phone link and no email link in the HTML a crawler receives — one prints a phone number as plain text a thumb cannot tap, two show nothing at all.

The 23 forms ask 4 to 21 fields, median 6: six ask 5 or fewer, eleven ask 6 or 7, five ask 8 to 11, and one asks 21. Of the fields I could read by name, email appears on 17 forms, name on 16, phone on 16, an address or ZIP on 8, a message box on 8, and the service needed on 3. Twenty-one of the 23 forms sit next to a tap-to-call link.

So: name, phone, email and what the job is — four to six fields, on the home page, next to the phone number. Our own contact section puts a booking calendar first and a five-field form under it with two fields required; the roofing demonstration’s estimate form asks four.

The form question is really a question about what happens in the thirty seconds after someone decides you might be the one. A homeowner with water on the floor calls. A homeowner planning a roof in the spring, reading at 10 p.m., does not — and if the only route is a phone number, that visit ends with nothing. The form is for the second person. The measurement below is of what the sites that already win the local map give both of them, and the verdict is short because the winners agree with each other more than the blogs do.

The measurement: 39 home pages, four ways to reach a business

On 16 September 2026 I fetched the home page of each of the 40 businesses in our 15 September sweep — the top ten map-pack results for plumber, roofer, HVAC and electrician in Grand Rapids — with curl, and read the HTML. A lead form is a <form> with at least two visible fields, so search boxes and one-field newsletter boxes do not count. The field count is the visible inputs, text areas and drop-downs in the site’s largest form, leaving out hidden fields, submit buttons, checkboxes and radio buttons. A contact link is any link whose address contains “contact”; phone and email links are tel: and mailto: as written. One electrical site did not answer, so the count is 39. Form, phone, or calendar: what each route is for

A form is one of three ways to turn a visit into a job, and each does a different job. The tap-to-call link is for the emergency: 32 of the 39 have one and none has a text link, and it belongs at the top of every page, above the form. The form is for the person who will not call yet, and its job is to be short enough to finish on a phone at night: long forms with addresses and file uploads are exactly what the top forty do not build. The calendar link is the third route and the one the sweep says is spreading — 19 of the 39 home pages carry a booking or “schedule online” link of some kind — and it beats “free estimate” as an offer because it names a time instead of asking for one. Six of the 39 also run a chat widget; that is a fourth route with a person behind it, and it costs a subscription the other three do not.

What a form should not do is stand alone. A homeowner who has just found water in the basement is not going to fill in five fields and wait; two sites in the top forty make them, and one prints a number a thumb cannot tap. The form answers the question “what if I am not ready to talk” — it does not replace the answer to “I need someone now”.

What this reading does not prove

  • Home pages only. A site whose only form is on its contact page counts as “no form on the home page” here, which is why the contact-link column is next to it.
  • It is a read of HTML, not of screens. Two roofers’ pages arrive without their content and cannot be counted; forms loaded inside an embedded frame were noted on two sites but their fields could not be read.
  • Which fields are required was not recorded. The counts are fields shown, not fields demanded.
  • No conversion data. This is what ranking sites do, not proof that a six-field form produced the ranking or a single lead. The case for a short form is that it is what the winners chose and that a longer one asks for more before offering anything.
  • Forty sites, four trades, one city, read once on 16 September 2026.

Common questions

Does a contractor website need a contact form if the phone number is already on it?

Yes — for the visitor who is not ready to call. Of the 39 top-ranked Grand Rapids trade home pages read 16 September 2026, 32 have a tap-to-call link and 23 also carry a lead form on the home page; 21 of the 23 forms sit next to the phone link. The phone is for the emergency; the form is for the person planning a job at night who will not dial. Keep both, with the phone first.

How many fields should a contractor contact form have?

Four to six. The 23 home-page forms on top-ranked Grand Rapids trade sites, read 16 September 2026, ask 4 to 21 fields with a median of 6, and 17 of them ask 7 or fewer. By name, email appears on 17 forms, a name on 16, a phone number on 16, an address on 8, a message box on 8 and a service drop-down on 3. Name, phone, email and one line about the job is what most winners ask; the rest is a question for the call.

Should a contractor website have a contact page or a form on the home page?

Both, but the home page matters more. Of 39 top-ranked Grand Rapids trade sites read 16 September 2026, 31 link to a contact page and 23 put a lead form on the home page itself; 13 rely on the contact page alone. A contact page is one more tap on a phone, so a short form on the home page — next to the tap-to-call link — reaches the visitor who would not have made that tap.
