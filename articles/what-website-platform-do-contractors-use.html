What website platform do contractors use? I checked 266 West Michigan trade sites: 130 run WordPress

Ask “what’s the best website builder for a contractor?” and page one, read on 23 September 2026, is the builders answering for themselves — Wix, Squarespace, Yola and a string of “best builders” lists — plus a forum thread of owners asking the same thing. None of them says what the contractors already ranking in a real local market actually use. So I looked under the hood of 266 of them.

By Matthew Kerr Published 23 Sep 2026 Updated 23 Sep 2026 9 min read
Quick answer

Mostly WordPress. On 23 September 2026 I read the homepages of the 266 working websites of the roofers, plumbers, heating and cooling companies, electricians, landscapers, painters and remodelers Brave Search lists in seven West Michigan towns, and identified the platform from each page’s own code. 130 (49%) run self-hosted WordPress; 82 (31%) are on a hosted builder — Wix 30, Duda 16, Squarespace 11, GoDaddy 10 and a handful of others; 11 sit on a marketing agency’s platform; 4 are a franchise’s national site; 9 are custom builds; 24 I could not identify.

The platform shapes some of what ends up on the page, in both directions. Hosted builders carried business schema more often — Wix 20 of 30, Squarespace 8 of 11, Duda 10 of 16 — than self-hosted WordPress (51 of 130), because builders tend to write that markup for you. WordPress sites were far more likely to show reviews on the page (36 of 130, against 0 of 30 on Wix and 1 of 16 on Duda), partly because review plugins are common there and partly because one HTML fetch cannot see a widget a builder draws by script. No platform guaranteed the basics. What matters more than the logo is that you own the domain and the account, can move the site, and put what a customer looks for on the first screen.

“Which platform?” is usually the first question an owner asks and the least important one to get perfect. Every platform on this list can hold a fast, clear, honest site, and every one of them also hosts sites that bury the phone number. The real questions are who controls the account, what it costs to leave, and whether whoever builds it puts the right things on the page. Counting what the local competition actually runs on at least takes the guesswork out of the first question.

The measurement: 266 trade websites, one homepage each

The sites come from the same 23 September 2026 reading as the roofing websites page: the first ten Brave Search local listings for seven trades in eight towns (Walker returned none), one fetch of each homepage with curl. A website listed under two trades counts once. Pages that were parked, blank, placeholders or someone else’s site are not in the 266. The platform is named from the page’s own fingerprints — a hosted builder’s asset address or generator tag (static.wixstatic.com, static1.squarespace.com, irp.cdn-website.com and the like), WordPress’s /wp-content/ paths, a framework’s build files — and the matching line is kept for every site in the receipts file. 21 different platforms turned up; 3 more sites name a tool of their own that is not on the list.

Three things stand out. WordPress is the default: 130 of 266, more than all the hosted builders together. Among hosted builders Wix leads with 30, and Duda — which describes itself as a platform for agencies, hosting companies and software vendors building client websites (duda.co, read 23 September 2026) — is next with 16. And the agency platforms (11) are a category of their own: the site lives inside the marketing company’s system, which matters the day you want to leave.

A page builder is neither good nor bad for a small business site. It is worth knowing which one you are on, because the builder, not WordPress itself, decides how hard the site is to edit later and how much code each page carries.

Two differences are big enough to mean something. Business schema: 20 of 30 Wix sites, 8 of 11 Squarespace and 10 of 16 Duda carry it, against 51 of 130 self-hosted WordPress sites — hosted builders tend to generate it from the business details you enter, while on WordPress someone has to add it. Reviews and FAQs run the other way: 36 of 130 WordPress sites show reviews and 18 of 130 carry an FAQ, against 0 of 30 Wix and 0 of 11 Squarespace sites showing reviews. Part of that is real — review and FAQ plugins are everywhere on WordPress — and part is this method: a builder that draws a review widget by script after the page loads shows one HTML fetch nothing, so the builder counts are floors. Either way, no platform guarantees the basics and none prevents them, which is why the checklist on the roofing page matters more than the logo in the footer.

What this reading does not prove

  • Fingerprints, not invoices. The platform is read from the page’s code; a site can hide it, and 24 could not be identified.
  • Brave’s listings, one day. These are the trade businesses one search engine lists in West Michigan on 23 September 2026.
  • Homepage only. Signals are read from the homepage HTML; a script-drawn element is invisible to one fetch.
  • Correlation, not cause. Nothing here says a platform makes a site rank or convert.

Common questions

What website platform do most contractors use?

WordPress. Of 266 West Michigan trade websites read on 23 September 2026, 130 (49%) run self-hosted WordPress, 82 use a hosted builder such as Wix (30), Duda (16) or Squarespace (11), 11 sit on a marketing agency’s platform and 9 are custom builds.

What is the best website builder for a contractor?

The one whose account and domain you own, that you can edit yourself, and that lets you put your phone number, service area, licence and reviews on the first screen. In the 23 September 2026 reading, hosted builders carried business schema more often than WordPress and WordPress sites more often showed reviews, but no platform guaranteed the basics and none prevented them.

Is Wix or WordPress better for a small business website?

Both run working trade sites in West Michigan: 130 of 266 read on 23 September 2026 run WordPress and 30 run Wix. WordPress moves between hosts and has more builders to choose from; Wix is simpler to run and harder to leave. Decide on ownership, editing and exit, not on the logo.
