A contractor site needs a page per service — 30 of the 39 top-ranked Grand Rapids trade sites link one, and plumbers and HVAC companies link a median 12 to 16

Page one for this question is nine web-design vendors and a forum thread, each with a number and none with a measurement. So I counted the service pages the businesses already ranking in Grand Rapids link from their home page, on 15 September 2026.

By Matthew Kerr Published 16 Sep 2026 Updated 16 Sep 2026 9 min read
Quick answer

One page for each job you want the phone to ring for, plus a home page and a contact page. Not one long page that lists everything: a search engine returns pages, and the page it returns for “water heater replacement Grand Rapids” is the one that is about water heater replacement, not the one that mentions it in a list of eleven services.

That is what the ranking sites do. Of the 39 top-ranked Grand Rapids trade sites I fetched on 15 September 2026, 30 link at least one service page from the home page and 20 link five or more. The median is 5. The split is by trade: plumbers and HVAC companies, who sell a list of distinct jobs, link a median 12.5 and 15.5; roofers and electricians link a median 2.

The nine sites with no service page at all still rank — in the map pack, which Google says is ranked on relevance, distance and prominence, not page count. Four of those nine also have neither a quote form nor a tap-to-call link on the home page. Page count is not what put them in the top ten, and their site is doing very little for them once a homeowner arrives.

Type the question into a search engine and every answer is a number somebody picked — five pages, seven pages, “beyond five” — from a company that sells pages. None of them looked at what the businesses already winning the search actually carry. This page reports one measurement, in one city, for the four trades that call me most, and then does the arithmetic for your own site.

What the 39 top-ranked sites link to

The set is the 40 businesses Google Maps ranked in its first ten for plumber, roofing company, HVAC company and electrician in Grand Rapids, read on the morning of 15 September 2026. Between 17:15 and 17:21 UTC that day each business’s own home page was fetched once with a plain HTTP request — no browser, no retries. One electrician’s site timed out at twenty seconds and is left out, which is why the denominator is 39 and not 40.

A service-page link, for this count, is any link on the home page to an address on the same site whose path contains /services/ or /service/, or a trade word — plumbing, roofing, hvac, heating, cooling, electric. Each distinct path counts once. So the number is how many service-shaped pages the home page points at. It says nothing about whether those pages are any good, and it cannot see a menu that JavaScript builds after the page loads.

Three things fall out of that table. First, a page per service is the ordinary thing, not the ambitious thing: 30 of 39 sites link at least one, and more than half link five or more. Second, the trades split into two shapes. Plumbers and HVAC companies sell a list of distinct jobs — a water heater, a drain, a sump pump; a furnace repair, an AC install, a maintenance plan — and their sites carry a page for each, which is where medians of 12.5 and 15.5 come from. Roofers and electricians in this set mostly sell one thing, and half of them link two service pages or fewer. Third, the very large counts belong to businesses that are bigger than a truck: the 47 is a national brand’s Grand Rapids location page, the 71 a company that does electrical and HVAC both. Those are not a target for a one-crew business, and I would not read the table as “more is better”.

The nine that link no service page at all

Nine of the 39 home pages link to nothing that looks like a service page: one plumber, three roofers, two HVAC companies and three electricians. They rank anyway — they are in the Maps top ten, which is where the set came from. That is worth sitting with before anyone sells you pages.

Google’s own help page on local ranking says “local results are mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity”, and describes prominence as “how well-known a business is”, based on “info like how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have” (support.google.com/business/answer/7091, read 16 September 2026). Page count is not on that list. A roofer with a long review history and a one-page site can hold a map-pack spot, and the site is not what put them there.

What the nine have in common is thinness everywhere, not only in page count. Four of the nine have neither a quote form nor a tap-to-call link on the home page, and five of the nine have no form. So the honest reading is not “pages do not matter”. It is that these businesses rank on something other than their website, and the website is doing very little for them: a homeowner who taps through from the map to a page with no way to ask for a quote goes straight back to the map. The tap-to-call article covers the fix for that half of the problem.

The map pack ranks the business. A service page is what ranks for the job.

Why a page per service, and not one long page

The reason is not a rule about page counts; it is the shape of the searches. Nobody types plumber and then reads a list. They type the job: water heater replacement, sump pump installation, furnace not turning on. A search engine returns pages, one per result, and the page it shows for “water heater replacement Grand Rapids” is the one whose title, heading and first paragraph are about water heater replacement in Grand Rapids. A home page that mentions eleven services in a bulleted list is about none of them.

That is what the plumbing and HVAC numbers are showing. The sites with a median 12 to 16 service pages are not padding; they have one page for each job they want the phone to ring for. Roofers and electricians can get away with fewer because the jobs are fewer — but “panel upgrade”, “EV charger installation” and “generator hook-up” are three different customers with three different searches, and an electrician with two pages is choosing which of them not to appear for. The same logic is why our contractor website page is built around a page per service and, for businesses that drive to several towns, a page per town.

One long page is not worthless. It is fine for the trade with one job, and it is far better than nothing. It simply cannot be about more than one thing at a time, and every service you add to it dilutes the one it was about.

The arithmetic for your own site

Write down the jobs you actually want more of — not everything you can do, the ones you want the phone to ring for. That number, plus a home page and a contact page, is the site. If you drive to several towns and want to appear for “roofer in Rockford” as well as Grand Rapids, add a page per town; that is a separate decision from services, and this sweep did not measure it.

What this measurement does not prove

  • Home page links only. A service page that exists but is not linked from the home page — or is linked only from a menu that JavaScript builds — is invisible to this count. Every number above is a floor.
  • A path is not a page. The check matches the address, not the content. A site with /services/plumbing/, /services/plumbing-repair/ and /services/plumber/ counts three whether or not those are three different pages, and town pages filed under /services/ would count too. That is part of why the largest counts are so large.
  • Ranking cause is not measured. The set is the Maps top ten. Nothing here shows that service pages moved anyone into it, and the nine sites with none show they are not required for it. What a service page earns is the search for the job, which the map pack does not measure.
  • One city, four trades, one morning. The roofer and electrician medians rest on ten and nine sites. Remodelers and landscapers were not measured, and their shape may differ.

Common questions

How many pages should a contractor website have?

One page for each job you want to be found for, plus a home page and a contact page. Measured on 15 September 2026, 30 of the 39 top-ranked Grand Rapids trade sites link at least one service page from the home page and the median is 5. Plumbers and HVAC companies, who sell many distinct jobs, link a median 12.5 and 15.5; roofers and electricians link a median 2. If you serve several towns, a page per town is a separate decision on top of that.

Does a contractor website need a page for every service?

For every service you want the phone to ring for, yes, because searches are per job and a search engine shows one page per result. A home page that lists eleven services is about none of them, while a page titled for water heater replacement can be shown for that search. You do not need a page for a job you would rather not do. The sites with the most service pages in the sweep are plumbing and HVAC companies, whose jobs are the most distinct from each other.

Can a contractor rank with a one-page website?

In the map pack, yes. Nine of the 39 top-ranked Grand Rapids trade home pages measured on 15 September 2026 link to no service page at all, and Google’s own help page says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and popularity, with prominence drawing on reviews and links. But a one-page site has no page to show for a specific job search, and four of those nine sites also had neither a quote form nor a tap-to-call link on the home page, so the site is doing little for them once a homeowner arrives.
