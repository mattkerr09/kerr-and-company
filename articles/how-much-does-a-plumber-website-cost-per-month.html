Running a plumber website costs about a dollar a month if you own it, $17 to $37 on a builder plan, or $199 with someone maintaining it — every price read on 16 September 2026

Page one answers the build price. The question a plumber asks after the build is what the thing costs every month, and every vendor answers with its own plan. So I read the purchase pages on one day and added them up per route, including the two vendors whose prices I could not read.

By Matthew Kerr Published 16 Sep 2026 Updated 16 Sep 2026 10 min read
Quick answer

About a dollar a month if you own the site and it is a static build: a .com domain is $11.08 a year at Porkbun and a static site hosts free on Cloudflare Pages, which says all plans come with unlimited bandwidth (both read 16 September 2026). Add $7 a month per mailbox if you want email on your own domain.

Seventeen to thirty-seven dollars a month if you rent a builder: Wix’s Light plan is $17 a month and Core $29.77, yearly subscription paid in full; GoDaddy’s Website Builder is “as low as $9.99 a month” on an annual term (wix.com and godaddy.com, read 16 September 2026). Squarespace’s prices could not be read from its pricing page today, so they are not quoted.

$199 a month if you want someone to keep it fast, secure and edited for you — that is our Care Plan, and it is optional. If you are paying off a Starter build on the $149-a-month plan, the payments run for twelve months and then stop, and the site is yours for good.

“How much does a plumber website cost” has a page-one answer, and it is the build: this site’s own arithmetic is in what a plumber should pay for a website. The follow-up question — and then what does it cost me every month? — gets answered by whoever is selling the plan. Wix quotes Wix. GoDaddy quotes GoDaddy. A web designer with a maintenance retainer quotes the retainer. None of them adds up the whole bill for a business that just wants the site to stay online and the form to keep arriving.

So this page does the adding, from the vendors’ own purchase pages, all read on the same day, with the routes side by side. Where a price could not be read that day, the page says so rather than guessing.

The spread is the point. The same plumber site can cost a dollar a month or two hundred, and the difference is not the site — it is who is responsible for it when something breaks, and whether the arrangement ends with you owning anything. Each line below is one route, with the receipt.

The domain: about a dollar a month

A .com at Porkbun is $11.08 a year, and its pricing page says “the price you see is the price you pay, year after year” with no first-year discount that jumps at renewal (porkbun.com/tld/com, read 16 September 2026). Divided by twelve that is 92 cents a month, and it is the one cost every route shares, because a domain is the thing you own whatever hosts the site. Registrars differ; I tried to read GoDaddy’s and Namecheap’s domain pages the same day and both refused the request, so their renewal prices are not quoted here. Check yours, and check the renewal price rather than the first-year one.

If you take nothing else from this page: register the domain in your own account, in your own name. Who owns your website and domain explains how to check, and why it matters more than any monthly figure below.

Hosting: free for a static site, with one sentence to read first

A plumber’s site is a handful of pages, a form and a phone number. Built as static files — which is how we build — it needs no server to run, and two of the largest hosts will serve it for nothing. Cloudflare’s Workers and Pages page says “all plans come with unlimited sites, seats, requests, and bandwidth” and “start building for free — no credit card required”; its Pages limits page gives the free plan 500 builds a month (cloudflare.com/products/pages/ and developers.cloudflare.com/pages/platform/limits/, both read 16 September 2026). Five hundred builds is five hundred edits; a plumber will not get near it.

GitHub Pages is the other free host, and it carries a sentence worth reading before you use it for a business. It is “available in public repositories with GitHub Free”, with a published site “no larger than 1 GB”, a “soft bandwidth limit of 100 GB per month” and a “soft limit of 10 builds per hour” — and its terms say it is “not intended for or allowed to be used as a free web-hosting service to run your online business, e-commerce site, or any other website that is primarily directed at either facilitating commercial transactions or providing commercial software as a service” (docs.github.com … about-github-pages and … github-pages-limits, read 16 September 2026). A brochure site with a quote form is not an e-commerce site or a software product, but that is GitHub’s sentence to interpret, not mine, and I would rather you read it than take my word. Cloudflare’s page carries no such clause.

What the free hosts do not include is anyone watching the site. If the form breaks at 2 a.m. nobody will tell you, which is the gap the care plan below exists to fill — and it is fine to fill it yourself by sending a test enquiry once a month.

Builders: $17 to $37 a month, on annual terms

If the site lives inside a builder, the builder’s plan is the hosting. Wix’s plans page, read from Grand Rapids on 16 September 2026, shows Light at $17 a month, Core at $29.77 and Business at $39.77, and states that “displayed prices are for yearly subscriptions, paid in full at the time of purchase” and that “prices and currency vary by location” (wix.com/plans). Light is the one that connects your own domain and removes Wix’s branding; each paid plan includes a free domain for the first year, and the page does not state what that domain costs to renew, so I have not. GoDaddy’s plans page lists its Website Builder “as low as $9.99 a month” with “33% savings with an annual term”, and Managed Hosting for WordPress “as low as $14.99 a month” (godaddy.com/websites/website-builder/plans-and-pricing, read 16 September 2026). “As low as” means the annual term at the lowest tier; the monthly-term price is higher and was not displayed.

Squarespace is missing from this list on purpose. Its pricing page, opened the same day, rendered the plan names and the annual-versus-monthly toggle but no figures that could be read as text, and I am not going to quote a price from memory. If you are choosing Squarespace, read the number on the page yourself and note whether the toggle is on annual.

The thing to understand about every builder figure is what stops when you stop paying. Your domain and your text are yours. The site — the pages, the layout, the form — is the plan, and it goes dark with it. That is not wrong, it is what renting is, but it is why the “about a dollar” route and the “$17 to $37” route are not the same product at different prices.

Email on your own domain: $7 a mailbox

Google Workspace’s pricing page lists Business Starter at $7 per user per month, with a 50%-off-for-three-months introduction showing on the read and an annual-commitment toggle on the page (workspace.google.com/pricing, read 16 September 2026). One mailbox is enough for most one-crew plumbers. It is optional — a free Gmail address costs nothing — but the quote form on a site called yourplumbing.com arriving at a yourplumbing.com address is the small thing that makes a business look like a business, and $7 is what it costs.

A dollar a month buys a site that stays up. Two hundred buys someone whose job it is to notice when it does not.

Maintenance: $199 a month, and what it is actually for

Our Care Plan is $199 a month for managed hosting and security, monthly edits and updates, and uptime and performance monitoring. It is optional on every build, and a static site can be handed to you to host free instead — that is written on the pricing, not a concession. What the plan buys is not hosting, which the table above shows is close to free; it is that someone else finds out first when the form stops arriving, the certificate lapses or a script slows the page down, and that the “change the hours for the holiday” email gets done without a quote. Does website speed matter has an example of exactly the kind of drift a plan is for: one script cost this site 24 points.

There is also the route where the monthly figure is the build. The Starter Site on our web design page can be paid as $149 a month for twelve months instead of $999 up front; hosting, updates and edits are included for those twelve months, and at the twelfth payment it stops and the site — code, content and domain — is yours. Stop early and the site comes down, we hold it for 90 days, and the buy-out stays open. That is a payment plan with an end, which is the thing to check on any monthly website offer: the handover checklist lists what you should be holding on the day it ends.

What this page does not prove

  • One day’s prices. Every figure was read on 16 September 2026 and vendors change plans without notice. Two showed promotions on the read (Wix’s “save 50%” badges on its higher tiers; Workspace’s three-month discount), and the underlying prices are what is quoted.
  • Two vendors unread. Squarespace’s figures did not render as readable text; GoDaddy’s and Namecheap’s domain pages refused the request. None is quoted, and none should be filled in from memory.
  • Location. Wix says its prices vary by location; these are the ones shown to a Grand Rapids visitor. Taxes are excluded everywhere.
  • Not included in any route: the build itself, photography, ad spend, call tracking, a booking tool, or any paid app you connect. Those are separate decisions and separate pages; the contractor website page lists what a build does and does not include.

Common questions

How much does a plumber website cost per month?

About a dollar a month if you own a static site and host it free: a .com domain was $11.08 a year at Porkbun, read 16 September 2026, and Cloudflare Pages hosts static sites free with unlimited bandwidth. Add $7 a month for a Google Workspace mailbox on your own domain. On a builder, the plan is the hosting: Wix Light was $17 a month and Core $29.77 on yearly terms, and GoDaddy’s Website Builder as low as $9.99 a month on an annual term, all read 16 September 2026. A managed care plan that keeps the site edited and monitored is $199 a month with us and is optional.

Is website hosting free for a small business?

For a static site, yes: Cloudflare Pages states that all plans, including the free one, come with unlimited bandwidth, and GitHub Pages is free on public repositories with a 1 GB site limit and a soft 100 GB-a-month bandwidth limit. GitHub’s terms also say Pages is not allowed to be used to run an online business, e-commerce site or software service, so read that sentence before hosting a business site there. A site built inside Wix, Squarespace or GoDaddy is hosted by the plan, so its hosting costs whatever the plan costs, on annual terms.

Do I need a website care plan, and what does one cost?

You need one if nobody in the business will notice a broken form, an expired certificate or a slow page until a customer mentions it. Ours is $199 a month (published on this site, read 16 September 2026) for managed hosting and security, monthly edits and updates, and uptime and performance monitoring, and it is optional on every build; a static site can be handed to you to host free instead. If you are paying off a Starter build at $149 a month, the payments run for twelve months and then stop, and the site is yours for good.
