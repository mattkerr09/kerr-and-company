How much does Google Business Profile management cost? Software from $30 a month, a person from $99 to $500 — what 24 pages actually say when you read them

Page one for this question is eight guides that each quote a range and, mostly, sell nothing at it. So on 16 September 2026 I read the guides, then the pricing pages of every seller they and two further searches led to, and wrote down only the numbers a business can actually pay.

By Matthew Kerr Published 16 Sep 2026 Updated 16 Sep 2026 10 min read
Quick answer

The profile itself is free — Google’s own page says so (business.google.com, read 16 September 2026). What costs money is someone managing it. Of the 24 readable pages I opened on 16 September 2026, seven sellers publish a plan price. Software that posts and answers reviews for you: $30 a month per location (renewlocal.com) and $99 a month per business (merchynt.com). A person managing one location: $99, $249 or $399 a month (gmbgorilla.com), $350 (mapranking.com), $499 (whitespark.ca), CAN$285 or CAN$475 in Canada (storytellermedia.ca), and from $500 a month here in Grand Rapids.

Of the eight guides on page one for the search, one states a price it sells at (the $99 software), one states an agency range of $300 to $1,200 a month (coloradowebimpressions.com), and six quote market ranges — $100 to $500, $125 to $400, $125 to $475 — with nothing to buy at those numbers.

Ours are on the Business Profile page: Managed Local from $500 a month per location with weekly Google Posts and Q&A, review requests and responses, and monthly rank and call reporting; a one-time Profile Tune-Up at $750; the Local Growth System from $1,200 a month. Cancel anytime, and you keep the profile and everything on it.

Every answer on page one to this question is a range, and a range you cannot buy is not a price. A business owner wants two things: the number a real seller will charge this month, and what that number buys. First, what the profile costs: nothing

Google’s Business Profile page answers its own FAQ in one line: “Yes, creating a Business Profile and listing your business on Google is free” (business.google.com/us/business-profile/, read 16 September 2026). Its local-ranking help page adds the part that matters for this question: “There’s no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google” (support.google.com/business/answer/7091, read 16 September 2026). So every dollar in the tables below buys labour or software, never placement. One of the page-one guides (optuno.com, read 16 September 2026) describes scam calls demanding $399 to “verify” a listing Google verifies at no charge; the profile has no fee, and anyone phoning for one is not Google.

The reading is not that the guides are wrong. Their ranges bracket the seller prices above almost exactly: the sellers’ per-location prices for a person run $99 to $500, and the guides say $100 to $500. The reading is that six of eight pages answering “how much does it cost” do not sell the thing, so the number you are given is a number nobody will invoice. When the same eight Grand Rapids SEO agencies were read for their own prices, one of eight stated one; this niche is more forthcoming, and it is still the sellers’ pages, not the guides, that carry the figure.

Why $30 and $500 are both “profile management”

The word covers two different purchases, and the price tells you which one you are looking at.

  • Software, $30 to $99 a month. Renew Local and Merchynt sell a program that writes review replies and schedules posts for you. You still decide what the business has to say; the software removes the chores and the forgetting. Renew Local’s own line is “10hrs+ Saved Per Month” (renewlocal.com, read 16 September 2026) — its pitch is your time, not a person’s.
  • A person, $99 to $500 a month per location. The cheapest human tier is watching, not writing: GMB Gorilla’s $99 “Monitoring” is daily checks, review responses and a monthly change report, with content updates starting at the $249 tier (one a month) and the $399 tier (four a month). Whitespark’s $499 and our $500 both put posts on a weekly cadence and add reporting; Whitespark adds a named account manager and competitor analysis, ours adds automated review requests after each job and Q&A.
  • Setup once, then you. GMB Gorilla’s one-time setup is $499 on its management page and $500 per profile on its setup page; our Profile Tune-Up is $750 once, with a citation audit and a review-strategy setup. Renew Local’s $750 is a different product — getting a new profile verified or a suspended one back, charged only on success.

One of the page-one guides is useful precisely because it describes the labour rather than the price: at minimum, “weekly Google Posts, photo uploads, Q&A response, review responses within 48 hours, attribute updates, and monthly insights review”, which it puts at “roughly 4–6 hours per month of skilled work” (coloradowebimpressions.com, read 16 September 2026). Five hours a month at a sensible hourly rate is the arithmetic behind every mid-range figure in the first table, and it is the question to ask any seller: how many hours, doing what, each month?

What this reading does not prove

  • These are list prices, not what anyone paid. Every figure is what a page displayed on 16 September 2026. Quotes, discounts and what a seller actually delivers for the money were not measured.
  • The set is who I found, not the market. Ten page-one results plus the sellers that two further searches and those results pointed to. A seller with a published price that none of those pages leads to is not here. Two Gumroad listings that came up could not be read as served HTML and are excluded rather than guessed at.
  • “Includes” is the seller’s list, not a check of the work. Whether Whitespark posts weekly or GMB Gorilla checks daily was not observed; the table repeats what each page says it does.
  • Currency and geography differ. Storyteller Media prices in Canadian dollars; Whitespark’s page says “All pricing in USD”; the rest are US. No conversion was applied.
  • Our plan is one of the seven. It is in the table at its published price with its published inclusions, no more and no less, and nothing here ranks it above the others.

Common questions

How much does Google Business Profile management cost per month?

On the pricing pages read on 16 September 2026, software that posts and answers reviews for you is $30 a month per location at Renew Local and $99 a month per business at Merchynt. A person managing one location is $99, $249 or $399 a month at GMB Gorilla, $350 at Map Ranking, $499 at Whitespark, CAN$285 or CAN$475 at Storyteller Media in Canada, and from $500 a month at Kerr & Co. in Grand Rapids. Setup on its own runs $499 to $750 one time on those pages. The eight guides on page one quote market ranges of roughly $100 to $500 a month, and six of them sell nothing at any number they quote.

Is Google Business Profile management worth paying for?

Only if the work gets done. The profile is free and Google says there is no way to pay for a better local ranking, so every dollar buys labour: posts, photos, review replies, Q&A, category and hours checks, and a report. One agency page read on 16 September 2026 puts the minimum at roughly four to six hours a month of skilled work. If you will do those hours yourself, $30 to $99 a month of software removes the chores. If you will not, a person at $250 to $500 a month per location is the going rate on the pages that publish one. Ask what is done each week, in writing, and whether you keep the profile if you stop.

Does Google charge for a Business Profile?

No. Google’s own page, read on 16 September 2026, says creating a Business Profile and listing your business on Google is free, and its local-ranking help page says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. Anyone who phones asking for money to verify or keep your listing is not Google; one page-one guide describes scam calls demanding $399 for a verification Google performs at no cost. The only money Google takes is for ads.
