How long does local SEO take? Only 2 of 8 Grand Rapids agency pages will tell you — and both say three to six months

Page one for this question is forum threads and agency blogs that all land on “it depends, usually a few months”. Nobody checked what the agencies a Grand Rapids business would actually call are willing to say about time on their own pages, so on 16 September 2026 I read them.

By Matthew Kerr Published 16 Sep 2026 Updated 16 Sep 2026 9 min read
Quick answer

What Google itself says about the clock

Google does not publish a timeline for local rankings. What it does publish, in its SEO starter guide, is this: “Every change you make will take some time to be reflected on Google’s end. Some changes might take effect in a few hours, others could take several months. In general, you likely want to wait a few weeks to assess whether your work had beneficial effects in Google Search results” (developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide, read 16 September 2026). And in its guidance on hiring an SEO: “An SEO audit should be about giving you realistic estimates of improvement, and an estimate of the work involved. If they guarantee you that their changes will give you first place in search results, find someone else” (developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/do-i-need-seo, read 16 September 2026). Hours to months, judged in weeks, promised by nobody. That is the whole of what the search engine will commit to, and it is more than six of the eight agency pages do.

The work can be done in month one. Its effect cannot.

Why it takes months: the four clocks

Local SEO is four different jobs whose results arrive on four different clocks, and an agency controls the pace of only the first.

  • The site. New service pages, fixed titles, schema, speed. This is the part that can be finished in weeks, and the part Google means by “hours to months”: the page has to be crawled, indexed, then judged against the pages already ranking. 30 of 39 top-ranked Grand Rapids trades link a page per service; building yours is month one, being ranked for them is not.
  • The profile and the listings. A complete Google Business Profile — categories, services, hours, photos, posts — and consistent listings in the directories that matter. The edits are quick; Google and the directories pick them up on their own schedules. Twelve free places to list a Grand Rapids business is the list, checked one by one.
  • Reviews. The slowest clock you can influence and the one the map pack weighs. On 15 September 2026 the twelve top-three map spots for four Grand Rapids trades held 22 to 12,142 reviews, and three of the twelve had under 60 (the reviews reading). Reviews arrive at the pace your customers leave them, which is a function of how many jobs you do and whether anyone asks. No agency can compress that below the number of jobs.
  • Links. Other sites deciding to mention yours. Slowest of all, least controllable, and the reason competitive trades take the long end of every range quoted above.

That is why a three-month number is not a dodge. It is roughly the time for the first clock to finish and the second and third to start showing, and it is why an agency that has done this before asks for three months up front and why our own SEO page does the same.

What this reading does not prove

  • The two timelines are claims, not measurements. Both agencies describe their own results in their own words; neither figure was checked against any client’s data, and this page does not say either is right.
  • Silence is not a policy. An agency that states no timeline on its page may give an honest one on the call. The measurement is what is written before the call, because that is what a business owner can compare.
  • One page per agency, served HTML only. A timeline on a services page I did not open, or added by a script after load, would be missed.
  • Page one, one engine, one day. Eight agencies is who a Grand Rapids business finds with that search on 16 September 2026; it is not the market.
  • No agency is being accused of anything. Every cell is what the named page said or did not say on that date, with the address in the receipts.
