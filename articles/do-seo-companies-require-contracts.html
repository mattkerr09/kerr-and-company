Quick answer

Often, and most will not say so on their website. On 16 September 2026 I read the Grand Rapids SEO page of the eight agencies on page one for the search. One states its term — month to month, no long-term contract. One states its own starting price. Six say nothing about either; the terms arrive on the sales call, after you have given your number.

A minimum term is not a scam. SEO takes months to show anything, so an agency asking for three to six months is asking for the time the work needs. What matters is whether the term is written down before you call, what happens to the pages, profile and reports you paid for when you leave, and whether anyone guarantees a ranking — Google’s own guidance says no one can.

Two readings. The first is that the question this article answers cannot be answered from the agencies’ own websites: seven of the eight say nothing about whether you will be asked to sign for six months or twelve. That is not the same as saying they require long contracts — a page that is silent proves silence, nothing more — but it means the term is a thing you learn on the sales call, which is the moment you are least able to compare. The second reading is that the one agency stating its term states it as month to month, and it is also the one that publishes a starting price. Whether that is a coincidence or a policy, it is what the eight pages show.

Why agencies ask for a term, honestly

SEO is slow. Pages have to be crawled, indexed and then judged against the pages already ranking; reviews and citations accumulate at the speed customers leave them. An agency that does the work in month one and is judged in month one will lose most clients before anything can show, and an agency that has been through that a few times starts asking for three or six months up front. That part is reasonable. Our own SEO page asks for a three-month initial commitment for exactly that reason, and then goes month to month.

The part to be careful about is what the term is protecting. A term that buys the work time is fair. A term that exists because the results will not survive scrutiny is not, and you cannot tell the two apart from the length. You can tell them apart from three other things on the page or in the proposal.

The length of the contract matters less than what you keep when it ends.

The three things to ask before you sign anything

  • What do I keep when I leave? The pages written for your site, the Google Business Profile (it should be in your Google account, with the agency as a manager, never the other way round), the analytics property, the citations, the reports. If the answer is “the pages are ours” or “the profile is under our account”, the term is not the problem. Who owns your website and domain is the same question one layer down, and the handover checklist lists the seven things to hold.
  • What happens at the end of the term? Month to month, or an automatic renewal for another full term? A notice period of thirty days is normal; a renewal that quietly re-signs you for a year is the clause people find out about in month thirteen.
  • Is anything guaranteed? Google’s own guidance on hiring an SEO says “no one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google” and, of an SEO who promises first place, “find someone else” (developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/do-i-need-seo, read 16 September 2026). A guaranteed position attached to a long term is the combination to walk away from, whatever the price.

What this reading does not prove

  • Silence is not a policy. A page that does not state a term may belong to an agency that works month to month, or one that asks for a year. The measurement is what is written before the call, because that is what a business owner can compare; it is not a survey of the contracts themselves.
  • One page per agency. A term or a price on a pricing page I did not open would be missed. Where the page linked to pricing, I followed the link; where it did not, I did not search the site.
  • Page one, one engine, one day. Eight agencies is who a Grand Rapids business finds today; it is not the market. The ranges two pages give for “Grand Rapids SEO” are their claims about the market, quoted as such, not measured by me.
  • No agency is being accused of anything. Every cell in the table is what the named page said or did not say on 16 September 2026, with the address in the receipts so you can read it yourself.

Common questions

Do SEO companies require contracts?

Many do, and most will not say so on their website. Of the eight Grand Rapids SEO agency pages on page one for the search, read on 16 September 2026, one states its term (month to month with no long-term contracts), one states its own starting price, and six say nothing about either. A minimum term of three to six months is common and reasonable because SEO takes months to show results; the questions that matter are what the term is protecting and what you keep when it ends.

How long should an SEO contract be?

Long enough for the work to show and no longer. Three months is enough to build pages, fix the profile and start the reviews and citations moving; six is defensible for a competitive market. After that the arrangement should go month to month with a normal notice period, not renew automatically for another full term. Our own SEO page asks for a three-month initial commitment and then goes month to month.

What should I keep if I leave an SEO company?

Everything they built with your money: the pages on your site, the Google Business Profile in your own Google account with the agency as a manager, the analytics property, the citations and the reports. Ask for that list in writing before you sign, because an agency that keeps the profile or the pages makes leaving expensive whatever the contract length says. And if any agency guarantees a ranking, Google’s own guidance says no one can, and that is the one to walk away from.
