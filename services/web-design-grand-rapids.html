Web development in Grand Rapids: what I build and what it costs

If you searched for a web developer, this is the part you want. I write every site by hand in HTML, CSS and JavaScript, so development is inside every website price above, not billed on top. When a site needs to do more than inform, I build a web tool into it: a quote calculator, a booking tool, or an intake form that feeds your inbox or calendar. One tool with a full site is the Growth package, from $4,999. A tool for a site you already have starts at $3,500, and several connected ones at $7,500. Customer logins and portals are quoted on their own. I don't build App Store apps; the app development page says why, and what a web tool does instead.

Development is also the part you cannot see in a screenshot: the code that makes a page load fast on a phone, send a form to the right inbox, and connect to the tools you already run. A good-looking page that scores 40 on a speed test, or loses its contact form to a spam filter, does not bring in business. I design and develop every site myself, so there is no hand-off between a designer and a developer.

Performance and mobile

Every page is built mobile-first and measured with Google's own tools before launch. Images are sized for the screen that loads them, fonts are limited to what the design needs, and there is no third-party script on the page that you did not ask for. On sites like the ten Grand Rapids contractor home pages I tested, a bad score usually comes from scripts loading up front or images far bigger than their space: a fix, not a rebuild.

Forms, calls, and tracking that arrive

A lead form is only useful if it lands somewhere you read. I test every form end to end, add click-to-call on mobile, and set up analytics so you can see which page produced the call. Of 321 West Michigan contractor and dentist sites I read, 120 had no working visit counter at all, so tracking goes in at launch, not later.

Structured data and clean markup

Search engines read a site through its code. I ship the schema markup that tells Google what your business is, where it is, and what it sells, and I keep the HTML simple enough that a crawler and a screen reader both understand it.

Hand-written, on hosting you own

My default build is plain HTML, CSS and a little JavaScript served from a content delivery network, the same way this site is built. No theme, no page builder, no plugins waiting for a security patch. A static site costs almost nothing to host and has little to attack. I set hosting up in your account and, if you want me to run it, the care plan covers updates, backups, and monitoring from $199 a month.

Website FAQs

How much does a website cost in Grand Rapids?

Here: $999 for a 1 to 3 page site, $2,499 for 5 to 8 pages with the copy written for you, and $4,999 for a site with a custom tool. Half to start and half at launch, or the Starter Site at $149 a month for 12 months, after which it is yours. Published Grand Rapids rates for a small business site run $3,000 to $15,000.

How long does a website take?

The build itself takes days: a five-to-seven page site is two to five working days of work. Projects stretch to months while everyone waits for content, so I write the site from one call instead of sending you a planner. The launch date is in writing before you pay.

Do we need to meet in person?

No. I work remotely: one recorded 20-minute call, then a link where you watch the pages appear. There is no office to visit, and the price is the same wherever you are.

Do you write the words on the site?

Yes. I draft every page from the call, your old site, your reviews and your listings, and you edit. Editing is faster than writing. You send job photos, or I use stock until you have them.

Can I see sites you have built?

Yes: three demonstration builds you can open on your phone, for a roofer, a restaurant and a salon. The businesses are fictional and each page says so. If you want a safer first step, the guarantee covers it: rework until you sign off, the launch date in writing, and a full refund if you cancel before I start.

Who owns the site when it is done?

You do. The code, the domain and the hosting account are in your name. If you leave, you take the whole site with you: no export fee, no rebuild. How to check who owns yours.

Do you work with WordPress, Wix or Squarespace?

Yes. If your site already runs on one and is doing its job, I work inside it. Of 266 West Michigan trade sites I read in September 2026, 130 run WordPress and 82 sit on a hosted builder such as Wix. For new builds I default to hand-written HTML on a content delivery network: faster, close to free to host, and no plugins to keep patched.

What happens after launch?

That is up to you. A static site can be hosted for free and has no plugins to keep patched, so you can run it yourself. If you want someone to host it, back it up and make your edits, care plans from $199 a month cover that.

Do you build for businesses outside Grand Rapids?

Yes. I build for businesses across West Michigan, including Holland, Grand Haven, Muskegon, Kalamazoo and the towns between, and for businesses anywhere in the US. The process and the price are the same.

What is the difference between web design and web development?

Design is how the site looks and reads. Development is the code that makes it load fast on a phone, send the form to your inbox and connect to your booking or quote tool. I do both on every build, so nobody hands a mock-up to a developer who then quotes you again.

Can the site rank in my town and not only Grand Rapids?

Yes. Local search runs on your Google Business Profile, the town named on your pages and in your schema, and listings that agree with each other. I point all three at the town your customers search from, and the local SEO service can manage it monthly.

The build doesn't change

The local setup does

Local rankings lean on three things you control: the Business Profile, the words on the page, and how consistently the business's name, address and phone appear across the web. For a Holland business I write Holland on the page and in the schema, set the Business Profile's service area to Ottawa County, and fix the listings so they agree. A Zeeland business should not have to compete for "Grand Rapids" to be found in Zeeland.

So does the season

Lakeshore and resort businesses do most of a year's selling in a few months. The site has to capture the July visitor who books immediately and the February planner who wants a quote emailed: an intake bot that takes the booking at midnight in July, a quote calculator that qualifies the lead in February. I build both paths, and the care plan keeps the site current between seasons.
