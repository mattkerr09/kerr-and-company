Exactly what the care plan covers

The plan is described on the pricing cards as keeping the site fast, secure and current: hosting, backups, monitoring, and monthly change requests handled for you. This is what each of those means in practice.

Hosting, security and backups

The site lives on hosting we manage, in an account you own, with the certificate renewed and the software kept current. Our default build is hand-written HTML, CSS and a little JavaScript served from a content delivery network, which has no plugin stack to patch and nothing to hack. On a site that runs a content management system, keeping the platform and its plugins updated is most of the security work, and exactly what that covers is agreed in writing per project. Backups are taken so that a bad edit or a failed update can be undone.

Monthly edits and updates

The reason most owners take the plan. You email the change — a new price list, holiday hours, a new stylist or technician, a photograph, a paragraph on the services page — and it is made, checked on a phone, and confirmed back to you. The number of change requests covered each month is set in writing when the plan starts, and anything beyond it is quoted before a minute of work happens. A new page or a new feature is not an edit; see below.

Uptime and performance monitoring

The site is monitored for whether it is up and how fast it loads, and we are notified when either changes. Most outages are quiet: a lapsed domain, an expired certificate, a hosting change nobody mentioned. Monitoring means we find out before your customers do, and the fix is usually made before you knew there was a problem.

Forms and tracking

Two things fail silently and belong in the plan's written scope: whether the contact form still reaches an inbox someone reads, and whether analytics still record. A spam filter changes, a mailbox is closed, a script is removed in an edit. A form that has not arrived for three months is the failure nobody notices until a customer mentions it in person, which is why what is checked, and how often, is agreed in writing per project rather than assumed.

What it does not cover

Being specific about this is what makes a $199 plan honest, and it is the part most maintenance pages leave out.

New pages, new features and redesigns. A page per service or a booking tool is a build, quoted separately at the published prices. A full rework is the redesign page.

Photography and logo design. You supply images and brand assets, or we use stock; logo work is quoted separately.

Your domain and third-party fees. The domain renewal, the booking or ordering software, a texting provider, any paid app the site connects to. We tell you what is due and when; we do not pay it for you.

Search engine optimisation beyond what is in the build. Ongoing SEO is a separate retainer, from $750 a month on the SEO page, and Google Business Profile management starts at $500 a month per location on the local SEO page.

Ad spend and ad management, which are on the digital marketing page.

Hosting and monitoring for a custom tool. A tool built alongside the site is managed separately, from $400 a month on the AI automation page, because software has more that can go wrong than a page does.

Regulated copy. Medical, legal or financial claims need a specialist to sign off, and an edit that introduces one is sent back to you for that.

What happens to a site nobody maintains

Nothing dramatic, at first. Then, one at a time, over a year or two, the ordinary things.

The domain renewal notice goes to the email address of the person who registered it, who has left, and the domain lapses. The site disappears, and so does every email address on that domain, on the same day. The security certificate expires and every browser shows a warning page before your home page; most people click back. A content management system and its plugins go unpatched, and the site is quietly used to host someone else's spam pages until Google flags it. The contact form's destination mailbox is closed, and the form keeps saying "thank you" to people whose messages go nowhere. The hours on the site are the old ones, the phone number is the old one, the price list is two increases behind, and the person who could change any of it cannot be found. The card on the hosting account expires.

Each of these is common, none of them is a design problem, and each costs customers in a way that never shows up as an invoice. The reason the care plan exists is that every item on that list is cheap to prevent and expensive to discover.

A site built by someone else: after the audit

We take on the maintenance of a site we did not build, but only after the Site & Business Audit: $750, paid once, with a written report in three business days. The reason is not a sales step. Promising to keep a site up means knowing what is in it — how it is hosted, what it runs on, whether the domain and the accounts are in your name, whether it is indexed, how fast it is, whether the forms arrive, and what its security state is. The audit scans every page against 190+ technical, SEO, local and conversion checks, including indexing, speed, security, schema and links, then a person reads your copy, offer and ads, and the report ranks what to fix by impact against effort with the exact fix.

The report is yours to keep, or to hand to anyone. Three outcomes are common. The site is sound, and we take it on with a care plan at the published price. The site needs specific fixes first, which are quoted in writing at fixed prices before any are made. Or the site is not worth maintaining and the honest answer is a rebuild, which the redesign page covers at the same packages and prices as a new build. If the site runs on WordPress, Shopify or Squarespace and is doing its job, we work inside it rather than move it. Whichever it is, the scope of the plan is agreed in writing per project before the first monthly payment.
