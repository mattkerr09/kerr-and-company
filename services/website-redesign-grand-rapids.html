When a redesign is the answer, and when it is not

It is the answer when

The site does not work on a phone, and most of your visitors are on one. The first screen gives the name of the business and a slogan and does not say what you do, for whom, or what to do next. Every service is on one page, so none of them can rank on its own. The site is slow because of a theme and forty plugins added over six years. It was built by someone who has gone, on a platform you cannot log into, and nobody can change the hours. Or the domain and hosting are in a previous designer's name, which is the case where a rebuild is really a rescue.

It is not the answer when

The local SEO page is blunt about the map pack: the ranking is driven mostly by your Google Business Profile — categories, completeness, review volume, consistent listings, activity — not by how good the website looks. If the profile is empty and the site is fine, a redesign changes nothing. The SEO page lists the boring, fixable reasons local businesses lose search traffic: no Business Profile, missing pricing and service pages, slow pages, and a name, address and phone that differ across listings. Some of those are pages to add, not a site to rebuild. A form that stopped arriving is a fix. An offer that says "free estimate" and fills the phone with tire-kickers is a copy problem, and why a calendar link beats a free estimate is the article for it. None of those needs a redesign.

What goes wrong in redesigns

Why website projects stall is the general case: build time is days, calendar time is months, and the gap is waiting on content. A redesign adds its own failures, and each is avoidable.

The content wait, again. The old site has copy, so there is no reason for a redesign to wait on a questionnaire. We draft from what exists and you correct it.

"While we are at it." Scope grows a page at a time until the fixed price is not fixed. The proposal names the pages, and anything added is quoted before it is built.

Trickled feedback. The article describes four hours of revisions spread across five weeks of texts and calls. Three rounds are included here, each in one place — click on the draft and type — with a date.

New addresses, no redirects. The most expensive one, and the one that shows up as lost rankings a month after launch. The redirect map is written before launch, not after.

The move that breaks email. A site moved to a new host without the domain's mail records being carried over, and the business's email stops the same afternoon. The fix is dull: list the domain's records before anything is switched, and check email still arrives afterwards.

The old designer holds the keys. If the domain or the hosting is in someone else's name, that is the first thing sorted, in writing, before the build starts.

Tracking and the form not carried over. Analytics, the pixel, the form's destination: each is checked on launch day rather than assumed, and a test submission of the form is the last thing done before the old site is switched off. If some of your customers are older or navigate by keyboard, the accessibility page lists what we test for on every page we publish, which is also worth knowing before a redesign that inherits an old site's habits.

Questions people ask about redesigns

Do I have to buy the audit before a redesign?

It is where we start, because it is the only way to know whether a redesign is what you need, and the $750 report is yours whatever you decide. If you already know the site has to be rebuilt, say so; the price is then fixed from the call, the same as any build.

Will I lose my Google rankings in a redesign?

Not if the addresses Google already knows are kept or redirected, which is part of every redesign here. Every existing address is listed before launch, kept where it can be and otherwise redirected to the page that replaces it. A launch that gives every page a new address and forgets the old ones is how rankings are lost, and it is the most expensive mistake on this page.

Can you redesign a WordPress, Shopify or Squarespace site?

When a site already runs on one of them and it is doing its job, we work inside it. For a rebuild our default is hand-written HTML, CSS and JavaScript served from a content delivery network, because it is faster, cheaper to host, and has no plugin stack to patch. If you need a store or an editor-friendly CMS, we say so in the quote.

Will you use my existing copy and photographs?

As raw material, yes. We rewrite the copy from your existing site, your Google Business Profile, your reviews and one call, and you correct the draft. Photographs are yours to supply; photography and logo design are not included, and a new logo is quoted separately.
